How Ratings Work

1307scout shows every statistic we can, so you can look at the numbers and draw your own conclusions. Ratings and the other derived stats on this page are there to supplement those numbers, not replace them. A grade is a quick summary to help you decide where to look, and the stats behind it are always shown next to it.

Two ratings, never blended

Every team can have two separate grades. Neither one knows the other exists.

Scouting ratingOfficial rating
Built fromMatch and pit entries from our own scoutsMatch results synced from FRC Events and The Blue Alliance
MeasuresWhat this robot did on its ownHow its alliances did, partners included
SeasonsThis season's form only (2026 REBUILT)Every synced match

They're kept apart so a team is rated on whichever data it actually has. A team with a full official record but no scouting coverage still gets an official grade, and when the two grades disagree, that's useful to see.

Letter grades

Both ratings turn into a letter the same way. Each team gets a score from 0 to 100, then the grade comes from the share of rated teams with the same score or lower. A grade is a ranking among the teams in our database, not a measure against all of FRC.

NRNot rated: fewer than 3 matches of that kind of data
TTest team: tagged by staff and left out of the grading

The scouting rating

A team's score is a weighted blend of seven parts, each from 0 to 100. These are the bars in the Rating breakdown on the Team Database. "Ranked" means the share of scouted teams at or below this team on that measure, so 80 means it's level with or ahead of 80% of them.

WeightPartHow it's measured
32FUEL scoringAverage FUEL points per match, ranked. Matches where the scout marked the role as Defense or Feeder are left out, so a good defender isn't graded as a bad scorer.
16ClimbingAverage TOWER points per match (auto and teleop), ranked.
16Reliability1 − (disabled rate + 0.6 × broke-down rate + 0.4 × stuck rate), never below 0. Not ranked.
11Shooting efficiencyThe average of two things: FUEL per volley (ranked) and scouted accuracy, where 1 to 5 maps to 0 to 100.
11Robot capabilityThe team's latest pit entry, scored in the table below. Not ranked.
9Autonomous80% average auto points (ranked) and 20% how often it left the starting zone.
5Fouls avoided1 − foul or card rate. Not ranked.

When data is missing

  • A whole part has no data: it's skipped and the other weights are rescaled, so a team isn't punished for, say, having no pit entry.
  • A field is blank in a match entry: FUEL and TOWER count as 0, and an unanswered problem such as "disabled" or "foul" counts as no.
  • Only Defense or Feeder matches: FUEL scoring has no data, so it's skipped.
Example. A team scores FUEL 80, Climbing 60, Reliability 90, Efficiency 70, Capability 40, Autonomous 50 and Fouls avoided 100.
(32×80 + 16×60 + 16×90 + 11×70 + 11×40 + 9×50 + 5×100) ÷ 100 = 71.2
With no pit entry, Capability is skipped and the remaining weights add up to 89:
(32×80 + 16×60 + 16×90 + 11×70 + 9×50 + 5×100) ÷ 89 = 75.1

The official rating

A simpler score, from results only:

Score = 60% × average alliance score (ranked) + 40% × win rate

For this rating a tie counts as half a win. Each match is also weighted by the tier staff set on its event, because results against a tougher field say more about a team:

Event tierMatch weight
FIRST Championship2.0×
District Championship1.5×
Qualifier1.0×
Off-season0.75×
Example. A team won 5 of 10 matches at a qualifier and 4 of 5 at a District Championship.
Win rate = (5×1 + 4×1.5) ÷ (10×1 + 5×1.5) = 11 ÷ 17.5 = 62.86%
If its average alliance score ranks at 70%:
Score = 0.6×70 + 0.4×62.86 = 67.1

Reading ratings carefully

  • Grades are relative. They depend on which teams are in the database, so a grade can change when other teams' data changes, even if this team hasn't played. Early in a season, with few rated teams, they mean very little.
  • Three matches is a low bar. A rated team may still have very little data behind it, so check the match counts.
  • A match scouted twice counts once. When several scouts enter the same team, event and match number, the rating averages their entries into one match, and if they disagree on something like "disabled", it counts as half. Entries with no match number can't be paired, so each counts on its own. Tables that say Entries, and the stats on the Alliance View, still use every entry, so they can differ slightly from the rating.
  • Scouted numbers are estimates. FUEL counts are approximate, and accuracy is a scout's judgment.
  • Official results include partners. A strong team on weak alliances can look worse than it is, and the reverse is also true.
  • Capability scores describe a robot's design, not how well it performs. A swerve drive scores higher than tank whether or not it's driven well.
  • Share codes are ranked differently. A guest's grades are worked out among only the teams in that share code, so they can differ from the Team Database.
  • Test teams don't affect real teams. They're ranked in their own group at every step, so a dummy team can't move anyone's score or grade. Teams with fewer than 3 matches are not graded, but they are still counted when each part is ranked.

Other derived stats

These appear on the Team Database and Alliance View. They use plain, unweighted numbers, so they won't always match the rating's inputs.

StatHow it's calculated
Win %Wins ÷ matches played. A tie counts as played but not as a win, and matches aren't weighted by event tier.
Qualification / Playoff win %The same, for just those matches. The number in brackets is the match count.
Avg score marginAverage of alliance score − opponent score, across wins and losses.
